Dust Math

The sticker CFM was measured with no hose at all. The hose decides what you get, the filter takes its cut, and the breaker gets a vote.

Open the app →

The hose truth

A 2.5 inch hose keeps about four fifths of the sticker CFM; the skinny 1.25 inch hose barely keeps half. Every foot past seven costs a little more.

The filter cut

A loaded filter keeps three quarters. A HEPA cartridge keeps 85 percent on its own. Suction dies at the filter, not the motor.

The circuit vote

The vac and the tool share one circuit, and the continuous line is 80 percent of the breaker. Past the line, the breaker votes no - run one motor at a time.

What it never does

It never believes the peak-horsepower sticker, never sells you a bigger vac, and never pretends the 4 inch hose fits your sander. The factors are labeled norms; your hose keeps the honest ledger.

Dust Math is open source - the engine is small enough to read, and the math is plain enough to argue with.
